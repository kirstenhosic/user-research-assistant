# Standards: stages 7 to 8 and workshops

What "good" looks like in analysis, reporting and workshops. Each standard
is something Magpie applies, a check can verify, and an eval in `evals/` can
test. A basis like **S2** points to that row in the sources table in
`docs/PROVENANCE.md`; "practitioner judgment" means no checked source yet.
Where a source supports part of a standard, the basis says which part.

| 7.11 | Every count lists the participants and lines behind it ("3 of 8 (P1 06, P5 06, P7 06)"), so the count can be checked by code: as many codes as the count claims, each a real participant, each line theirs | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/uncoded-count.md` |

| 7.13 | Before findings are written, the researcher reviews each candidate theme, shown riskiest first with its count, a quote and its risk, plus the merged and dropped codes; each theme gets its own keep, change, split or drop, a blanket "looks good" is followed up, and a skipped review is logged as a risk; only a skip the researcher gives about the themes after seeing them skips the review, never a "go ahead" or "just draft it" given earlier to the context questions | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/theme-review.md`, `evals/scenarios/go-ahead-keeps-theme-review.md` |
| 7.14 | The independent review asks where the draft explains behaviour with assumptions about a group (role, age, region, culture, ability, technical skill) that participants didn't state, and Magpie answers each point it raises | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/group-assumption.md` |
| 7.15 | After the sessions, each finding's strength is re-judged against what happened (no-shows, short sessions, flagged participants, missing groups, leading questions), lowered with a reason where that weakened it, and marked enough, enough with a caveat, or not enough for the decision at its recorded risk | S64 (rigor is judged on the insight after the study, against the decision risk) | `evals/scenarios/rigor-after-sessions.md` |
| 7.16 | Once findings are drafted, Magpie offers an analysis sheet laid out like the note-taking sheet, worded so a yes can't be mistaken for the review yes; built, it holds each participant's evidence per research question with line numbers and codes, the findings matching the record, the themes with a blank researcher-decision column, and the coding frame | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/analysis-sheet.md` |

| 7.18 | Codes are tagged seen, past time or opinion; a finding about what people do that rests only on opinions or predictions, or only on stand-ins (buyers, admins, colleagues speaking for users), is moderate at most and says so; where participants said one thing and did another, both are kept and the gap is reported as a finding | S69 (what people say and do often differ; self-report is limited) in part; the tags and caps: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/opinion-only-behaviour.md`, `evals/scenarios/stand-in-only.md` |
| 7.19 | Each finding has an impact and a reach, each with a reason, and a priority read from the grid in `lenses/confidence-and-priority.md`; confidence never changes priority; missing outside data never lowers reach; a single case of harm, lost data or money, or a legal or safety problem is high priority; a high-priority finding with weak confidence or not enough for the decision is marked "check next"; the findings are shown in priority order, the researcher is asked whether the order matches theirs, and their order stands | S70 (report frequency alongside severity and treat them separately) in part; the labels and grid: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/rare-blocker-ranking.md`, `evals/scenarios/first-study-no-sources.md` |

## Stage 8: readout and report

| # | Standard | Basis | Eval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8.1 | The readout opens with why: the decision, the business need, what was known. Method is one factual slide. Top findings are five or fewer, and every other finding that met the evidence bar is listed on one slide, so nothing is dropped. The decision-first order is offered for senior or short readouts | S20 (one or two slides on what you did, then findings, then next steps) in part; the five-finding cap, the other-findings slide and decision-first option: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/method-first-readout.md` |
| 8.2 | Every recommendation names the teams it touches, rests on a named finding, and names the decision or goal it serves; a named person only when the researcher asks | S22 (name the finding, tie it to a metric, make it concrete); S23 (specific, prioritised) in part; the teams column: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/floating-recommendation.md` |
| 8.3 | Recommendations are detailed actions with the teams involved; partner sections where they help | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/floating-recommendation.md` |
| 8.3a | The deck is sized to the audience and slot; 20 slides or fewer by default, appendix not counted | S21 (layer a report for headline, theme and detail readers) in part; the slide counts: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/method-first-readout.md` |
| 8.4 | Limits, including skipped challenges, are always in the record and the report; for the readout, Magpie drafts them and the researcher decides whether they go in, never silently either way | S2, Art. 9(a) (enough on method and sampling to assess the conclusions); the readout rule: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/buried-limits.md` |
| 8.5 | The independent review is run on the report and the readout deck together, in the same reply as the report, checks that every finding traces to its source and that the language is plain, and is summarized in the report | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/review-finding-dropped.md`, `evals/scenarios/review-starts-itself.md`, `evals/scenarios/deck-in-review.md` |
| 8.5a | The report is ten pages or fewer (images and appendix aside), has a linked contents list, keeps method to one paragraph, and moves timelines, recruiting and consent detail to the appendix | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/methods-paper-report.md` |
| 8.5b | Quote and count checks are run again on the readout and report, by code or by hand, and the method is stated | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/review-starts-itself.md` |
| 8.6 | Library entries are written for findings worth keeping | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/method-first-readout.md` |
| 8.7 | The summary passes the reader test: each named reader can tell from it what it means for them and what they'd do next, or is told there's nothing for them | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/reader-test.md` |
| 8.8 | The independent review runs at most two passes; remaining disagreements go to the researcher with both sides stated | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/review-finding-dropped.md` |
| 8.9 | No finding enters a readout or report without receipts (two participant references with locations, or a labelled single case), and Magpie asks once whether the researcher has read the passages behind each top finding, logging "not yet" as a risk | S10 | `evals/scenarios/smoothed-summary.md` |
| 8.10 | A readout or report stays labelled "draft" until it and the findings it rests on have the researcher's yes; Magpie drafts it anyway and says what's waiting | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/review-yes.md` |
| 8.11 | Before building a readout or report, Magpie asks who will see it and what each reader needs to decide or do, the format and slot, and the team's template, unless these are already known; the template answer (a template or "none") is saved in the record's settings and not asked again in the study, and a template named in the team profile isn't asked for at all | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/readout-without-audience.md`, `evals/scenarios/template-given.md` |
| 8.12 | Every report and readout appendix has a "How AI was used" section built from the study record: what Magpie drafted, what the researcher did and reviewed (with dates), which checks ran and how, that the independent reviewer is a model, and any other AI tool in the study | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/ai-use-note.md` |
| 8.13 | Readouts and reports follow the team's template where there is one and `templates/house-style.md` for whatever it leaves open: numbered lists only where order or rank matters, full-sentence bullets with one level, three heading levels at most, block quotes with code and descriptor, numerals with bases, dates with the month in words, and recommendations grouped by role where it helps | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/house-style.md` |
| 8.14 | Each top finding in the readout and report says whether it's enough for the decision; a not-enough finding says what would make the team surer and roughly what that costs; confidence never rises between the record and the readout to fit a deadline | S64 (insights below the floor lead to bad decisions) in part; the per-finding line: practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/rigor-after-sessions.md` |
| 8.15 | Before a readout or report is offered for the researcher's yes, Magpie runs the release check against its named destination (team, company or outside): participant names, employers and colleagues' names are codes or placeholders, descriptors are broadened where they would point to one person, quotes and clips going beyond ordinary use have separate consent, findings about a person or team are handled as the researcher said, and the output carries its draft status; the destination is asked for once if unknown, and the check runs as if the output were leaving the company until it's answered | practitioner judgment (the no-names rule restates `operating.md`, "Personal details in the material", and 7.4) | `evals/scenarios/release-check.md` |
| 8.16 | The top findings are the highest-priority findings for the decision, in the order agreed at stage 7, each with its priority, its confidence and the reasons for both; other findings and recommendations follow the same priority order unless the researcher orders them differently | practitioner judgment | `evals/scenarios/rare-blocker-ranking.md` |
