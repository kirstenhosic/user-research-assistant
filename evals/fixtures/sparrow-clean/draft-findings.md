# Draft findings: Sparrow shift swaps study

Six interviews, October 2026: five managers (P1, P2, P3, P4, P6) and one
server (P5), from restaurants, a café, a bar and a hotel restaurant.
Research question: how do teams arrange shift swaps today, and where does
it go wrong? Decision: whether to redesign Sparrow's swap feature next
quarter. Draft by the researcher, before review.

**Finding 1. Most swaps are agreed in a group chat, outside Sparrow.**
4 of 6 participants described swaps being agreed in a staff group chat
(P1 04, P2 04, P4 04, P6 04); among the five managers, 4 of 5
(P1, P2, P4, P6). Not fitting: P3's
team swaps through Sparrow's swap feature, under a rule P3 set (P3 04,
06, 08), and P5 arranges swaps by texting a coworker directly (P5 04).
Strength: moderate.
> "Honestly, it's the group chat. Always the group chat." — P1

**Finding 2. Managers lose track of swaps agreed in the chat.** 3 of 6
said the rota falls behind the chat and they lose track of who is working
(P1 08, P2 08, P4 08). Not fitting: P6 keeps track by correcting a printed
rota as swaps happen (P6 08). P3's swaps go through the app, so the
problem doesn't arise for P3. Strength: moderate.
> "The rota says one thing and the chat says another, and the chat wins
> because that's what people read." — P2

**Finding 3. An unrecorded swap led to a missed opening (single case).**
1 of 6: a swap never reached the rota and nobody opened the café (P2 10).
One participant, kept because a missed opening is serious. P4 had two
people turn up for one shift (P4 08), a different failure, and said
overtime comes from sickness rather than swaps (P4 10). Strength: single
case.

**Finding 4. Two managers want to approve swaps before they count.** 2 of
6 (P1 12, P6 10), both managers. P3 already approves every swap in the
app (P3 06). The other managers didn't raise it, which says nothing either
way. Strength: weak.

**Also noticed. Some people don't know the swap feature exists or don't
use it.** P5 didn't know there was one (P5 08). P1 has seen it but says no
one on the team uses it (P1 10). Two participants, different reasons.
Strength: weak.

No participant mentioned notifications. That is a fact about these
sessions, not evidence that notifications don't matter.

## Recommendations

1. Explore bringing swap requests and manager approval into Sparrow, so
   the rota and the agreement are the same thing. Rests on Findings 1, 2
   and 4. Confidence: moderate. [assumption] Teams that use chat today
   would move; P3's team did, but only under a manager's rule (P3 08,
   10), and that is one team.
2. Before redesigning, find out why the existing swap feature goes unused.
   Rests on the "also noticed" finding. Confidence: weak; two
   participants.
