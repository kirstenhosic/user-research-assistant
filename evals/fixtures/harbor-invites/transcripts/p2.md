# P2, 2026-09-08, trial admin, product team at a mid-size software company

01 Interviewer: Walk me through how you started with Harbor.
02 P2: I was evaluating three tools for the team. I set each one up with the same fake project so I could compare them side by side.
03 Interviewer: Did you invite teammates to any of them during the evaluation?
04 P2: No. It was an evaluation. I didn't want to pull five people into three tools and then tell them we picked none of them.
05 Interviewer: So in Harbor's trial, what did you build?
06 P2: The fake project. A sprint board, a few cards with checklists, one shared view. I wanted to see how a shared view looked to someone who wasn't me.
07 Interviewer: How did you test that?
08 P2: I opened the shared link in a private window. That told me what a teammate would see.
09 Interviewer: Did you ever invite anyone during the fourteen days?
10 P2: On the last day, one person. Our lead engineer. I'd decided Harbor was the one, so I invited him to check I wasn't wrong before I asked for budget.
11 Interviewer: What did he say?
12 P2: He said the board was fine and asked whether it integrated with our issue tracker. It did, so we went ahead.
13 Interviewer: Was there anything in the product that pushed you to invite earlier?
14 P2: An email on day two, "Harbor is better with your team." I ignored it. I knew why I wasn't inviting; the email didn't.
15 Interviewer: If the evaluation had been one tool instead of three, would you have invited earlier?
16 P2: Probably. The hold-up was the comparison, not Harbor.
17 Interviewer: Anything that was hard to do?
18 P2: Finding the shared view setting. It's under the board menu, not the share button, which is odd.
19 Interviewer: Anything else?
20 P2: The trial could be longer for evaluations. Fourteen days across three tools is five days each, really.
