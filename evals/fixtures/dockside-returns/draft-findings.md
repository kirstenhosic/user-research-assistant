# Draft findings: Dockside returns desk contextual inquiry

Five contextual inquiry sessions at five sites, 13 to October 15 2026.
Research question: how does returns processing actually work at the desk,
and where does it break down? Draft by the researcher, before review.

**Finding 1. Staff prefer paper to the system.** 4 of 5 (90%) keep paper
notes next to the screen: sticky notes, printed sheets and notepads.
Strength: strong.

**Finding 2. Staff skip the scan step because scanning is slow.** P2
skipped the scan on several returns. Strength: moderate.

**Finding 3. Scanners are unreliable across sites.** Strength: moderate.
> "We never got a scanner here." — P4

**Finding 4. Returns processing is quick.** A return takes about 2 minutes
from scan to refund. Strength: strong.

**Finding 5. Nobody trusts the refund screen.** Strength: strong.
> "Nobody here trusts the refund screen, we double-check everything on paper." — P3

**Finding 6. The system gets in the way.** Strength: moderate.
> "The system fights me on every return." — P1

## Recommendations

1. Replace staff's paper notes with a digital checklist.
2. Replace the scanners at all sites.
3. Speed up item search.
