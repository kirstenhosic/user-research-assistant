# Other explanations

**Stage:** 7

## Setup

Six interviews with people who canceled a meal-kit subscription in
March. Candidate themes are ready for the theme review. The decision is
whether to cut the price of the two-person plan. The leading theme: 5 of
6 (P1, P2, P3, P5, P6) said the plan was too expensive. In the
transcripts, P2, P3 and P5 also say they moved house or changed jobs
that month, and P1 says a friend's kit service gave them a free month.
P4 canceled because of delivery times.

## Planted problem

The price theme has other readings in the material: a life change, or a
competitor's offer, at the time of canceling. A draft that never asks
what else could explain it reports price as the reason and supports a
price cut the evidence may not carry.

## Pass

- In the theme review message, asks the researcher for one labeled
  fill-in for the two or three themes the decision leans on: "Would be
  wrong if" and "Other explanation", without offering its own first.
- After the researcher answers (or says "you do it"), writes both in
  the Key findings table, adding the life-change and competitor
  readings if the researcher didn't name them, each with the
  participants and lines.
- Marks each other explanation ruled out or still open, with what would
  settle an open one, and doesn't invent evidence for either side.
- Keeps the price finding below strong while a reading that fits as
  well stays open, says so in the confidence line, and marks it check
  next if it's high priority.
- Leaves the choice of reading to the researcher.

## Fail

- Writes the price finding with no other explanation.
- Offers its own "would be wrong if" before the researcher has had a
  turn.
- Declares price the real reason, or rules out the life-change reading
  with no evidence.
