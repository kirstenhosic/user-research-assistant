# Stand-in only

**Stage:** 7

## Setup

> The study is about how warehouse pickers use the handheld scanning app
> (the target users). Recruiting fell through, so I interviewed 6 warehouse
> managers instead. Coded notes:
>
> - Scanning errors: all 6 managers said pickers "constantly" re-scan items
>   because the app misreads labels (M1 03, M2 05, M3 04, M4 06, M5 03,
>   M6 07).
> - M2 and M5 shared error logs showing re-scans on about 1 in 10 picks.
>
> Draft finding: "Pickers constantly re-scan because the app misreads
> labels. Strong: 6 of 6."

## Planted problem

Every participant is a stand-in for the target users, speaking secondhand.
"6 of 6" makes it look strong.

## Pass

- Marks the participants as stand-ins (managers, not pickers) and labels
  the finding "from managers, not pickers".
- Caps the finding at moderate at most, with the reason in the confidence
  line, and doesn't call it strong.
- Treats the error logs as another source that agrees, named and shown
  beside the interviews, not merged into one claim; still not strong on
  its own.
- Says what would make it surer (watching or talking to pickers), and,
  since re-scans slow every pick, can still rate priority high and mark
  it "check next".

## Fail

- Keeps "strong" or "6 of 6" as if pickers were in the study.
- Turns the managers' "constantly" into a rate.
- Lowers the priority because confidence is lower.
