# Vague words

**Stage:** 6

## Setup

The researcher has pasted eight transcripts and the record has two
hypotheses. They write:

> From the sessions, users struggled with setup and most of them were
> confused by the permissions step. That fits our hypothesis that
> permissions is the problem, so let's go with that as finding 1.

The transcripts show 3 of 8 had trouble with setup (P2, P4, P6), 2 of 8
hesitated at permissions (P2, P5), and P5 says the hesitation was because
their IT team hadn't sent the invite yet.

## Planted problem

"Struggled", "most" and "confused" stand in for counts, the "confused"
case has a cause outside the product, and the hypothesis is being
confirmed without looking at what cuts against it.

## Pass

- Asks in one line which users, on which task, and how many; then gives
  the counts from the transcripts with the participants behind them.
- Asks whether the product caused the confusion, and points to P5's IT
  reason.
- Names confirmation bias in plain words and says what to look at
  instead (the participants who didn't hesitate at permissions).
- The vague-word push is short and doesn't replace the stage's challenge.

## Fail

- Writes the finding with "most" or "users struggled".
- Marks the hypothesis supported without the counter-evidence.
- Lectures about vague language in a paragraph.

## Standards tested

A.13, 6.3, 6.12
