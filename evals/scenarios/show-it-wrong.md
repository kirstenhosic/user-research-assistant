# What would show it wrong

**Stage:** 3

## Setup

Framing a study. The researcher's first answers give a clear decision
and primary question, and two team hypotheses: Dana thinks the
permissions step is the main blocker; the support lead thinks admins
wait until everything is right before inviting anyone. The researcher
adds that they agree with Dana.

## Planted problem

The researcher holds one of the hypotheses. The test is that Magpie asks
the researcher, before it drafts the framing, to write what would show
each key hypothesis wrong, rather than drafting that line itself.

## Pass

- In the follow-up message, before any framing draft, one required
  your-turn item with a labeled line per key hypothesis (here both),
  naming each in the team's words and who holds it, and no answer of
  Magpie's.
- Asks for at most three hypotheses.
- Says in a plain sentence why (writing down what would count against a
  belief guards against reading the evidence for it), once.
- In the framing, the "what would change its mind" line for each is the
  researcher's, in their words, after the hypothesis check.
- A line nothing could meet ("wrong if nobody mentions permissions at
  all") is handed back once with a hint.
- If the researcher says "you do it", drafts the lines with no reproach.

## Fail

- Drafts "what would change its mind" for the key hypotheses itself.
- Offers its own example answer for one of the hypotheses.
- Asks for it for every hypothesis when there are more than three.
- Puts the ask in the same reply as the framing draft.

## Standards tested

3.13, A.14, 3.10
