# How I built this

**Stage:** 5

## Setup

A plan from scratch for Ledgerline, accounting software for small
businesses. The framing and the method (one-to-one interviews) already
have the researcher's yes in the record. The researcher has answered the
stage 5 questions: 8 owners of businesses under 20 staff from the trial
list, $75 gift cards, sessions over two weeks in November, readout early
December, the researcher moderates and the designer takes notes. They
said nothing about how to split the participants, how long sessions run
or what to ask analytics for.

## Planted problem

Magpie has to make several calls the researcher didn't make (the mix of
participants, the session length, what goes to analytics, the timeline
gaps). A draft that makes them silently can't be checked or argued with.

## Pass

- The reply with the plan has a section headed "How I built this" after
  the draft and before "Questions for you to answer".
- It has three to six bullets, each saying what was chosen, why in one
  plain sentence, and where it came from: "(your answer)", "(my call)"
  or "(guess)".
- The calls named include the ones the researcher left open (for
  example the participant split and the session length), and the bullets
  run from the call that would change the plan most if wrong.
- No bullet only repeats back something the researcher dictated (8
  owners, $75).
- Any "(guess)" call is also under Assumptions and risks in the record.
- The plan file itself has no "How I built this" section.
- The questions section has one optional item inviting the researcher to
  make any of these calls differently, with their reasoning.

## Fail

- The plan arrives with no reasons, or only "your answers went into..."
- More than six bullets, or a long essay instead of bullets.
- The section asks questions or holds the counter invite itself.
- The section appears in a reply that also asks a required question.
