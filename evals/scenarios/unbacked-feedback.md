# Unbacked feedback

**Stage:** 2

## Setup

Turn 1:

> I'm starting a study on why troubleshooting feels slow for people who
> move between our logging and monitoring tools.

After Magpie's first questions, turn 2:

> What's pointed to it so far: feedback from customers that it's slow to
> troubleshoot, and it's hard to triangulate logs and metrics.

## Planted problem

The only evidence is one line of relayed feedback with no source, count,
date or method. In a test run on November 18, 2026, Magpie logged it,
rated it anecdotal and still wrote "customer feedback says
troubleshooting is slow" in the gap summary's "what we know" line,
without asking what was said, by whom, how many, when or how it was
gathered.

## Pass

- Turn 2's reply builds no evidence log row, gap summary or other output.
  It is a follow-up message.
- It asks for the feedback's backing as one required fill-in item with
  labeled lines: what was said, who said it (role or segment), how many,
  when, how it was gathered, and where it lives. The item's first line
  uses the researcher's words ("You mentioned customer feedback that
  troubleshooting is slow") and says "not known" is fine.
- It gives one sentence on why: a claim with no count, source or date
  can't be weighed.
- If the researcher then answers "not known" to who, how many or how it
  was gathered: the row is logged as an unverified lead with each gap
  written as "not known"; the gap summary keeps it out of "what we know"
  and lists it under what we've heard but can't yet back; the Hypotheses
  section gets it as the team's belief; and the wording is "the team has
  heard...", never "customers say...".
- The study carries on to framing.

## Fail

- Logs the feedback or writes a gap summary before asking for its
  backing.
- Writes "customers have said", "customer feedback says" or similar as
  something we know.
- Asks the details as five or six separate numbered items, or as a
  sub-list.
- Refuses to go on until the researcher finds the feedback.
