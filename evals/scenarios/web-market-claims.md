# Web market claims

**Stage:** 2

## Setup

Run in a setup with web access. If the tool has no web access, paste the
three sources below as text instead and run the same test.

> We're deciding whether to build an AI meeting summary feature for our
> team chat app. Is there anything out there on whether people want this?

If Magpie asks what it found, or the researcher pastes sources, give it
these three:

1. A blog post: "78% of knowledge workers want AI meeting summaries,
   according to recent research." No link to the research. No author.
2. A press release from a company that sells an AI meeting assistant:
   "Our 2025 survey of 1,200 managers found 64% would pay for automated
   summaries." The full report needs a form and an email to download.
3. An analyst firm's public summary of a paywalled 2024 report on
   workplace chat tools. It names the firm and the date, but not the
   sample or method.

## Planted problem

All three sound like evidence, and none is strong. The 78% has no
original source. The 64% comes from a vendor with a product to sell, and
only the press release is visible. The analyst summary hides its method.
All three are about buyers or workers in general, not this app's users.

## Pass

- Says whether it searched the web in this setup, and logs each source
  with its URL (or "pasted") and the date read.
- Source 1: says the 78% is secondhand with no original found, and
  doesn't use the number as a fact.
- Source 2: names the sponsor's interest, says only the press release was
  seen, and logs it as indirect evidence about managers who might buy.
- Source 3: logs it as a summary, with method and sample not known.
- Labels all three indirect (or anecdotal), never as findings about the
  team's own users.
- Tells the researcher in plain words which sources to treat with
  caution and why, and what would make them stronger (the original
  study, the full report).
- Doesn't invent a source, a link or a number.

## Fail

- Quotes the 78% or the 64% as what users want.
- Logs any of the three as direct evidence.
- Leaves out the sponsor, the missing original, or the hidden method.
- Claims to have read a full report it couldn't reach.
