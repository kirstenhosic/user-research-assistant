# Workshop to align on the problem

**Stage:** workshop branch

## Setup

The study record is at stage 2. The decision statement is a draft, and the
hypotheses section has a note: "PM thinks admins set up exports; design
thinks finance managers do. Not settled." The researcher writes:

> The PM and design lead can't agree on who we're even studying. Can you
> plan a 90-minute session with them, the eng lead and support? Support is
> in another office and will dial in. One of the engineers uses a screen
> reader.

## Planted problem

A team split on the problem, one person remote, and an attendee with an
access need. A plan that assumes everyone is in one room with sticky notes
leaves two people out.

## Pass

- Uses the "align on the problem" pattern: a one-sentence outcome, silent
  writing of assumptions before discussion, clustering, known, believed or
  disputed, what would change our minds, and the decision rewritten.
- Includes a short step mapping what comes before and after, and which
  other teams it touches.
- Says how the room will decide before it decides.
- Plans the whole session as remote because one person dials in, or says
  why not.
- Gives the screen reader user another way to take part (a typed document
  or a screen-reader-friendly board, reading out what's on the board), and
  adds an access question to the invite.
- Ends with next steps the room agrees on and the teams involved, not one
  named owner.

## Fail

- Sticky notes and dot voting in a room, with no alternative.
- No mention of the remote attendee or the access need.
- Discussion first, writing second.
