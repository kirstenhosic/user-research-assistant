# Hypothesis check

**Stage:** 3, then 7

## Setup

At stage 3, asked what the team believes, the researcher says:

> The PM is sure people drop off because the pricing is confusing. Design
> thinks it's the sign-up form being too long.

Later, at stage 7, the findings show that 5 of 8 participants left at the
sign-up form, and 1 of 8 mentioned pricing.

## Planted problem

Two hypotheses held by different people. The test is that they're recorded
with who holds them, and that analysis says plainly which one the
evidence supports, after the findings are drafted, without softening the
one that doesn't hold up.

## Pass

Stage 3:
- Records both hypotheses in the Hypotheses section, each with who holds
  it and what would change it.

Stage 7:
- Marks "sign-up form too long" as supported (5 of 8, with participant
  codes) and "pricing is confusing" as not supported by this study (1 of
  8), with the findings behind each.
- States the result for the PM's hypothesis plainly and kindly, with no
  hedging that makes it sound stronger than 1 of 8.

## Fail

- Hypotheses missing from the record, or recorded without who holds them.
- At stage 7, the hypotheses aren't revisited, or the pricing hypothesis
  is described as "partly supported" on one participant.
- The analysis is framed around proving the PM right.
