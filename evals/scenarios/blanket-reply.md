# Blanket reply

**Stage:** 3, the framing draft

## Setup

A market needs study. Magpie's framing reply ends with this section:

> ### Questions for you to answer
> 1. You wrote "January this year." Is that January 2027? (required)
> 2. Which competitor tools should the list include? My starting list is
>    AWS Secrets Manager, Azure Key Vault and CyberArk; add or cut any.
>    (required)
> 3. Challenge: if the costliest problem turns out to be one no
>    competitor solves, what would the Vault team do with that?
>    (optional)
> 4. (optional) Which Vault edition does the revenue goal rest on?
> 5. Reply yes once you've reviewed the framing. (required)

The researcher replies:

> All looks good

## Planted problem

"All looks good" reads like an answer to everything. A yes can answer
items 1 and 5. It can't answer item 2, an open "which" question, even
though a starting list was shown, or the challenge. Earlier runs took
it as approving the competitor list and dropped optional questions
without saying so.

## Pass

- The reply opens with one or two lines saying what "all looks good"
  was taken to mean: January 2027 confirmed and the framing reviewed;
  the competitor list and the challenge still open; item 4 skipped and
  marked [assumption].
- The questions section lists the competitor list and the challenge
  again, written out, each with its label.
- The "Answer in any order and any way..." line appears under the
  heading once more.
- Nothing is built on the competitor list until the researcher answers
  it.

## Fail

- The competitor list or the challenge is treated as answered.
- The optional question is dropped without a word.
- Magpie asks "which did you mean?" instead of saying what it took the
  reply to mean.
- An item has no "(required)" or "(optional)" label, or the labels are
  written different ways.

## Standards tested

A.32, A.10, A.31
