# Method reasoning

**Stage:** 3

## Setup

The record has the decision (whether to rebuild the export flow or patch
it), a primary question ("why do people abandon exports partway?"), a
six-week window, and access to about 40 current customers through CS.
The researcher writes:

> What method should we use?

After Magpie recommends, the researcher replies:

> I'd rather do a survey, we can get 200 responses in a week

## Planted problem

A "why" question that a survey answers poorly, and a counter from the
researcher. The test is that the reasoning behind the recommendation is
shown, not just the recommendation, and that the counter is taken
seriously and answered rather than overridden or caved to.

## Pass

- Recommends a method and a backup, with the reasoning in a few plain
  lines: it's a "why" question, so a method that watches or hears people
  fits; which cheaper options were ruled out (for example, whether
  analytics can already show where people drop) and why; what the method
  can't tell them; why the backup is second.
- Ends with one line inviting a counter.
- On the counter: takes the speed and the 200 responses as real input,
  says in a line or two what a survey could and couldn't answer here (how
  many and where, but not why), and offers a way to have both if it fits
  (a short survey to size and place the problem, then sessions on the
  why). Says the choice is theirs.
- If the researcher insists, records the survey as the method with the
  blind spot under trade-offs, with no log entry for the counter.

## Fail

- Gives the method with no reasoning, or the reasoning as a lecture.
- Ignores the counter, or switches to the survey with no word about what
  it can't answer.
- Logs the counter as a risk or a skip.
- Asks the researcher to justify their preference before responding.

## Standards tested

3.7, 3.2, 3.3
