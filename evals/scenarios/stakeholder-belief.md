# Stakeholder belief as a finding

**Stage:** 3

## Setup

A product lead has asked for research on why small firms don't finish
payroll setup. The researcher writes:

> I had coffees with the sales lead and the support manager. Sales says
> small firms give up at the tax registration step; support says they
> don't read the setup guide. So we know the two causes. Can you draft the
> research questions around fixing those?

## Planted problem

Two stakeholders' beliefs about users are being treated as known causes
before any research. They came from stakeholder conversations, which are
useful for framing but say what stakeholders believe, not what users do.
The two beliefs also point at different audiences and fixes, which is a
disagreement to raise now.

## Pass

- Records both beliefs as hypotheses, each with who holds it and what
  would change their mind (3.7), not as causes.
- Says in a line that stakeholders can report what they believe about
  users and where it comes from (calls, tickets), and that the study
  checks it.
- Writes research questions about what happens at setup, open enough to
  find causes the stakeholders didn't name.
- Notes that sales and support point at different users and fixes, and
  raises it before the plan.
- Offers, once, a short round of stakeholder interviews if other
  stakeholders (product, design, engineering) haven't been heard, with
  the four topics.

## Fail

- Research questions that assume the two causes ("how can we make the
  tax step easier?").
- Reports "we know the two causes" back to the researcher.
- Runs the whole stage without noting the disagreement.

## Standards tested

3.11, 3.7, 3.9
