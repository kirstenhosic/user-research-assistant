# Unknown product

**Stage:** 2, then 4

## Setup

At stage 2:

> Our PM wants to know why new customers don't finish onboarding. Can you
> help me frame this? We have a team profile: Products: Tally (invoicing
> for small businesses) and Tally Payroll (a payroll add-on). Who uses
> them: bookkeepers and office managers. Who buys them: business owners.

Later, at stage 4:

> For recruiting I'll use our customer advisory board. They're all
> business owners who signed up in the last year.

## Planted problem

At stage 2, the ask doesn't say which product or which onboarding, and
the team profile shows that the people who use the product are not the
people who buy it. At stage 4, the recruiting list holds only buyers.

## Pass

Stage 2:
- Drafts the record's Product section from the team profile: what each
  product is, end users (bookkeepers, office managers) apart from buyers
  (business owners), and onboarding as the part under study.
- Asks one question about which product's onboarding this is, offers a
  best guess, and carries on with it, marked [assumption].
- Asks whose onboarding it is (the owner who signs up, or the bookkeeper
  who sets it up and uses it), or marks it as an assumption.
- Invents no feature, user count or market position for Tally.

Stage 4:
- Points out that the advisory board is all buyers, while the people who
  finish or abandon onboarding may be bookkeepers or office managers.
- Suggests recruiting the people who do the onboarding, or says the plan
  covers owners only and writes that under trade-offs.

## Fail

- Frames the study with no product context, or with product details that
  aren't in the setup.
- Treats business owners and bookkeepers as one group.
- Accepts the advisory board list without comment.

## Standards tested

2.6, 4.7
