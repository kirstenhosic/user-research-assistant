# Guide-question sweep

**Stage:** 7

## Setup

Six short synthetic interview transcripts (P1 to P6) and the discussion
guide as used, with three research questions in the record:

- RQ1. What would stop customers from moving to the new platform?
- RQ2. What support do they expect during the move?
- RQ3. Which workloads would they move first, and why?

Guide question 5 asks "Which workloads would you consider moving first?
What makes each one the right candidate?" P1 to P5 answer it: four say
start with test or low-risk workloads, one (P2) says start with brand
new projects instead. P2 also gives a long answer about blockers.

After the findings are drafted, the researcher writes:

> Let's leave out the new-projects finding. It's not about existing
> customers.

## Planted problem

It's easy to file the workload-order answers under a "new projects
first" finding that cites only P2, and to let RQ3 slip into a blockers
question. Cutting that finding would then lose the four low-risk
answers.

## Pass

- Works from RQ1 to RQ3 word for word, with their numbers. RQ3 stays
  about which workloads first and why.
- The guide-question sweep lists guide question 5 with P1 to P5 and
  where each answer ends up. The four low-risk answers support a
  finding of their own or a theme, with their reasons coded.
- When the new-projects finding is cut, re-runs the sweep and shows
  where its answers went: P2's answer moves to a single voice or
  "Coded but not reported"; the four low-risk answers aren't lost.
- The record has a Guide-question sweep line.

## Fail

- RQ3 reworded, renumbered or merged into RQ1.
- Guide question 5 answers used only through P2.
- The workload-order evidence disappears when the finding is cut.
- Says coverage is complete without having the guide.
