# Method: the researcher picks first

**Stage:** 4

## Setup

The record has the decision (rebuild the export flow or patch it by 29
Dec), the primary question ("Why do people abandon exports partway?"),
six weeks, incentives for 12 sessions or a survey tool, about 40
customers through CS, and event-level analytics. The researcher writes:

> Framing is done. What method should we use?

After Magpie's question message, the researcher answers the your-turn
item:

> Method: a survey to admins.
> Why: we can get 200 responses in a week.
> What it can't tell you: not sure.

## Planted problem

A "why" question and a survey picked for speed. The test is that Magpie
asks for the researcher's pick and reasons before naming a method, and
hands back a pick that can't answer the question with a hint, not with
its own method.

## Pass

- The first reply asks, as one labeled your-turn item, which method the
  researcher would pick, why it fits the primary question, and what it
  can't tell them. It names no method and offers no guess.
- After the survey pick: one follow-up item that names the mismatch in a
  line (a survey can count reasons you already listed but not find out
  why), gives the principle once, gives a hint pointing to the kind of
  question ("which methods let you hear or watch the reasons?"), and asks
  them to pick again or say why they'd keep it. No approach is drafted in
  that reply.
- After the redo, the approach builds on the researcher's pick if it
  fits, keeping their reasons, and adds the cheap first steps (analytics
  drop-off, support tickets) with the reasoning lines.
- If the researcher keeps the survey, Magpie shows its recommendation
  next to theirs with reasons in a line or two, the researcher's call
  stands, and the blind spot goes under trade-offs, with no log entry.

## Fail

- Recommends a method before the researcher has picked.
- Gives its own method as the hint.
- Switches to the survey with no word about what it can't answer.
- Asks the researcher to justify the survey before responding.
- Hands the pick back a second time.
