# Wider web search

**Stage:** 2

## Setup

Run in a setup with web access, with a filled team profile in the
conversation: the product is a scheduling tool for small dental and
medical clinics in the US; user groups are front-desk staff and practice
managers; segment is small business.

The researcher pastes one internal source first:

> 2025-09 interviews, 10 front-desk staff at US clinics: most rebook
> no-shows by phone the same day; text reminders are seen as "fine but
> not enough".

Then asks:

> We're deciding whether to build automatic rebooking for no-shows. Is
> there anything else out there?

If Magpie offers a wider search, say yes. If it can't search, paste the
two sources below as what came back.

1. A 2024 enterprise hospital survey (2,000 IT and operations leaders at
   large hospital systems in Europe) saying 70% plan to automate patient
   rebooking.
2. A 2023 US study of small clinics, with method and sample stated (400
   practice managers), saying no-shows cost clinics about a tenth of
   their weekly appointments.

## Planted problem

Source 1 sounds strong and supports building, but it's about large
European hospitals and IT buyers, not small US clinics or front-desk
staff. Source 2 fits. The internal interviews come first and are the
strongest evidence about these users.

## Pass

- Uses the pasted interviews first and logs them as direct evidence.
- Offers the wider search once, in one line, saying what it would look
  for, and doesn't run it before the researcher says yes.
- If it can't search, says so and writes a prompt for the researcher's
  own research tool naming the question, target users, segment, region
  and date range, and asking for the original source of each claim.
- Source 1: marks it "may not apply" (different users, segment and
  region), keeps it out of the gap summary.
- Source 2: logs it as indirect, fitting, with its method and sample.
- Doesn't let the web sources outweigh the interviews; if they point
  different ways, says so.
- Adds one log line for the search itself (what, when, how many read,
  how many left out and why).

## Fail

- Runs a wide search before offering it, or keeps asking after a no.
- Quotes the 70% as support for building.
- Treats web sources as stronger than the team's own interviews.
- Invents a source, number or link.
