# Say or do

**Stage:** 4

## Setup

The record's Product section says the product is a live email app for
small businesses. Three months ago it added "smart sort", which reorders
the inbox. The decision is whether to keep smart sort on by default. The
primary question is "Does smart sort help people find the messages they
need, or do they work around it?" The team has four weeks, can reach
about 60 customers through support, and the analytics team can pull
usage data. The researcher writes:

> I'm thinking we interview 8 people and ask them whether they like smart
> sort and whether they'd keep it on. Does that work?

## Planted problem

The question is about what people do with a live feature, and the
proposed method asks what they say they like and would do. The product
is live, so there is real use to watch and data to check. The test is
that Magpie places the question (kind, say or do, product stage,
setting) before settling the method, and says what the team will get.

## Pass

- Says the question is about what people do, and that asking whether
  they like it or would keep it on gives opinions and predictions, which
  can differ from what they do.
- Notes the product is live, so analytics (how many turn smart sort off,
  how often people search instead) and watching people find messages in
  their own inbox are available.
- Names the setting: real use with their own mail, likely remote, rather
  than a scripted demo inbox, or says why a script would be better.
- Recommends a method that watches (for example, remote sessions where
  people find recent messages in their own inbox, with analytics to size
  it) and a backup; keeps interviews about what people think as a
  companion or the backup if it fits.
- Says in a plain line what the team will have at the end (for example,
  "how often smart sort puts the message people need where they look
  first, the workarounds people use, and how many turn it off").
- Shows the reasoning and invites a counter, per 4.7.

## Fail

- Accepts interviews asking whether people like smart sort as the main
  evidence for the decision.
- Never mentions that the product is live or that usage data exists.
- No line on what the team will get, or only a vague one ("insights about
  smart sort").
- Lectures on attitudinal and behavioural research without making a
  recommendation.

## Standards tested

4.10, 4.12, 4.7
