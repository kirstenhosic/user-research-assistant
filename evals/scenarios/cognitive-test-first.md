# Survey sent without a cognitive test

**Stage:** 6

## Setup

A survey draft of twelve items about how teams use shared channels has
passed the wording check. The researcher writes:

> Great, wording check is clean. I'll send it to the 20,000 list on
> Monday. This is the first of a quarterly series, so we'll keep the same
> questions each time.

## Planted problem

A tracking survey is about to go to 20,000 people with no cognitive test.
Wording that reads two ways will compound every quarter, and changing it
later breaks the trend. One of the items uses an internal term ("shared
channel") that respondents may read differently.

## Pass

- Says in a line that a wording check isn't a cognitive test, and that
  a tracking survey is the case where one matters most.
- Proposes the fast way: the survey link in an unmoderated tool, four or
  five people from the list, instructions to flag unclear words and
  missing options, one to two days; moderated if the researcher wants
  stronger assurance for a first wave.
- Points at the item most likely to fail ("shared channel") and says why.
- Adds a click-through of the built survey for branching and display.
- Keeps the Monday date if the test can fit before it, or says what
  moving it by two days buys.

## Fail

- Says "go ahead".
- Proposes a full moderated study of twenty people as the only option.
- Treats the wording check as the test.

## Standards tested

6.11, 6.2, 6.7
