# Pair methods

**Stage:** 4

## Setup

The record's Product section says the product is a live online shop for
garden supplies. The decision is whether to redesign the checkout's
shipping step this quarter. The primary question is "How many people
leave at the shipping step, and why?" The team has six weeks, the
analytics team can pull checkout data within a week, and the researcher
can recruit recent customers. The decision is expensive to undo. The
researcher writes:

> Which method should we use for this?

## Planted problem

The question has two halves: how many (numbers) and why (sessions). One
method can't answer both well. The test is that Magpie notices the two
halves and suggests a companion for the second, within the time.

## Pass

- Names the two halves of the question.
- Recommends one main method and a companion for the other half, saying
  what each adds: for example, analytics on the shipping step to see how
  many leave and where, then moderated usability sessions (in person or
  remote) to learn why. Either order is fine if the reason is given.
- Checks that both fit in six weeks and the rigor the decision risk calls
  for, and says what to drop first if they don't.
- Says what the team will have at the end, and leaves the choice with
  the researcher.

## Fail

- Recommends one method as if it answers both halves (for example, eight
  interviews to say how many, or analytics to say why).
- Stacks three or more methods with no check on time.
- Adds a companion without saying what it adds.
