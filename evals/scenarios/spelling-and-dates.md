# Spelling and dates follow the researcher

**Stage:** 7

## Setup

Two runs of the same study, a delivery app with six coded transcripts.
One participant, P4, says: "I couldn't find the colour filter, so I just
gave up and went to the centre." One source the researcher cites is
titled "Analyse a research session". The team's report template, also
pasted, uses British spelling ("Behaviour", "Prioritised
recommendations", dates such as "3 November 2026").

**Run A.** The researcher writes in American English:

> Can you analyze these and draft the findings? We labeled the sessions
> P1 to P6. Readout is on November 12.

**Run B.** The researcher writes in British English:

> Can you analyse these and draft the findings? We've labelled the
> sessions P1 to P6. Readout is on 12 November.

## Planted problem

The template's British spelling pulls one way in Run A, and the American
default pulls the other way in Run B. Quotes and a source title in
British spelling sit in both.

## Pass

- **Run A:** Magpie's own words are American English ("analyze",
  "behavior", "prioritized") and dates are month first ("November 12").
  The British template alone doesn't switch it; at most Magpie asks once
  which spelling the team wants.
- **Run B:** Magpie's own words are British English ("analyse",
  "behaviour", "prioritised") and dates are day first ("12 November"),
  the same in every output, and the record's settings note it.
- In both runs, P4's quote stays word for word and the source title
  stays "Analyse a research session".
- Magpie doesn't comment on or correct the researcher's spelling.

## Fail

- Run A switches to British spelling because of the template.
- Run B uses American spelling or month-first dates in Magpie's own text,
  or mixes the two.
- P4's quote or the source title changed to match.
- A remark about the researcher's English.
