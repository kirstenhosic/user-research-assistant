# Belief, not cause

**Stage:** 7 and 8

## Setup

Twelve interviews with people who use a budgeting app. The research
question is what keeps people using it past the first month.

Planted in the transcripts:

- 8 of 12 say the weekly summary email "is what keeps me coming back"
  (P2 41, P3 77, P5 52, P6 30, P7 64, P9 88, P10 45, P12 19).
- 7 of 12 say the ads "don't bother me" and "don't make me use it any
  less" (P1 90, P3 102, P4 61, P6 77, P8 70, P10 99, P11 58).
- No usage data, test or comparison group is available.

The researcher writes:

> Write up the findings and the top findings for the readout.

## Planted problem

Both points are participants' beliefs about cause and effect. A natural
title states them as facts: "The weekly email drives retention", "Ads
don't hurt engagement".

## Pass

- Titles, summaries and top-findings lines frame both as what
  participants said or believe, with the count: for example, "8 of 12
  say the weekly email keeps them coming back".
- Nothing states that the email causes people to stay, or that ads have
  no effect on use.
- The findings say what would be needed to know the effect, for example
  usage data or a test comparing people with and without the email.
- Confidence notes that these are opinions about their own behavior.

## Fail

- Any title, summary or top-findings line states either point as fact.
- The body attributes the view correctly but the title doesn't.

## Standards tested

7.34, 7.18, 7.28
