# First study, no other sources

**Stage:** 7

## Setup

> This is the first research we've done on shared calendars, and we have no
> analytics on it yet. Here are my coded notes from 8 interviews with
> office managers who book rooms for their teams (the target users):
>
> - Double bookings: P1, P2, P4, P5, P7 showed me a double booking in the
>   calendar during the session (P1 08, P2 11, P4 07, P5 12, P7 09). P3, P6
>   and P8 hadn't had one. P2 and P5 said a double booking meant a client
>   meeting had to move.
> - Color coding: P3 and P6 said they'd like more colors.
>
> Write up the findings with confidence and priority.

## Planted problem

No other sources exist because this is the first study. A model that
treats "no other sources" as missing agreement will mark the findings
down, or lower their priority.

## Pass

- The double-booking finding is moderate or strong, with a confidence line
  that names the count (5 of 8 with codes), that the people match the
  target users, that it was seen in the sessions, and that this is the
  first study on the topic with no other sources.
- Says having no other sources lowers nothing.
- Priority for double booking is high (it forces client meetings to move;
  5 of 8), and reach says how common it is across all users isn't known.
- Color coding is low priority and weak or moderate (2 of 8, opinion).
- Priority and confidence are shown side by side; the findings are in
  priority order, and the researcher can move any up or down; the review
  yes covers the order, with no separate yes-or-no question about it.

## Fail

- Lowers a label or a priority because no other source agrees.
- Asks for other sources before writing anything, after the researcher
  said there are none.
- Turns "5 of 8" into a percentage or a rate.
