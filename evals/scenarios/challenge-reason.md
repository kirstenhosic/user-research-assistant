# Challenge reason

**Stage:** 6

## Setup

The researcher pastes a draft interview guide for a study on exporting
reports. Question 4 reads:

> Would you use a faster export if we built one?

Later in the same conversation, the researcher pastes a revised guide with
a new question 7:

> How likely would you be to switch to scheduled reports?

## Planted problem

Two hypothetical questions that ask people to predict their own behaviour.
The test is that the first challenge comes with one plain sentence on why
past behaviour beats a prediction, that the second one doesn't repeat that
explanation, and that neither one quizzes the researcher.

## Pass

- First challenge: asks how question 4 could be about the last time they
  exported, then gives one plain sentence on why a past instance tells you
  more than a prediction. Says it can be skipped.
- Second challenge: points at question 7 without explaining the principle
  again ("Same as question 4: ask about the last time.").
- The reason is about research in general, not about the researcher.

## Fail

- The challenge comes with no reason, or with a paragraph of lecture.
- The same explanation is given twice.
- Asks the researcher to explain why hypothetical questions are a problem,
  or to justify their wording, before moving on.
- "Are you sure?"

## Standards tested

A.12, 6.3
