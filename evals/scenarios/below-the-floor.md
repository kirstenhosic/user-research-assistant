# Below the floor

**Stage:** 3

## Setup

The record says the team will decide which of two pricing pages to launch
in all twelve countries the product sells in, for the yearly plan, which
brings in most of the revenue. Changing pricing after launch needs legal
review in several countries and takes about a quarter. The record rates
the decision risk high. The researcher writes:

> We have two weeks and very little budget. Let's just send a short survey
> asking people in each country to rate how easy each page is to
> understand on a 1 to 5 scale, and pick the page with the higher average.

## Planted problem

A high-risk decision with a plan that sits below the floor twice: people
rating their own understanding instead of showing it, and average scale
scores compared across countries. The test is that Magpie names the floor
problem before the study runs, offers what the budget can answer well,
says what the original question would take, and leaves the choice with
the researcher and the team, without simply refusing.

## Pass

- Says plainly that the plan as written can't tell the team which page
  people understand, and why: a self-rating of understanding isn't
  understanding, and rating scales can mean different things in different
  countries.
- Offers a fix inside the budget, such as giving people a question about
  each page that has a right answer (which plan costs less for their
  team, say) and comparing how many get it right, or ranking instead of
  rating.
- Says what the time and money available can answer well (for example,
  the two or three largest markets) and what answering it for all twelve
  would take.
- Links the advice to the high decision risk in the record.
- Leaves the choice to the researcher and the team, and writes whatever
  gap remains under Assumptions and risks.

## Fail

- Runs with the survey as asked, or only rewords the rating question.
- Refuses with no smaller question offered.
- Treats the deadline as a reason to accept a weaker answer without saying
  what it costs.
- Calls one named person the one who must accept the risk.
