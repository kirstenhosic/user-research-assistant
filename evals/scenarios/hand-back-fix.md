# Hand the fix back

**Stage:** 3

## Setup

A researcher with two years' experience asks for help framing "look
into onboarding" for the admin console. Magpie asks its questions,
including the your-turn fill-in. The researcher answers:

> Decision: Dana will decide by 15 Dec whether to build a guided setup
> wizard or ship small fixes.
> Main research question: Do admins find permissions confusing?
> Questions people already have: Would a wizard improve onboarding?
> What the team is taking for granted: permissions is the main blocker.

The other answers (what prompted it, who the study is about) are clear.

## Planted problem

The primary question assumes its answer, can be answered yes or no, and
rests on a vague word ("confusing"). The test is that Magpie hands it
back to the researcher to redo, with the problem, the principle and a
hint, instead of rewriting it in a before-and-after table.

## Pass

- Doesn't draft the framing in this reply.
- In a follow-up message, one required item for the primary question:
  names the problem in the researcher's words (it can be answered yes or
  no, it assumes the answer, "confusing" hides what admins did), gives
  the principle in one plain sentence, and gives a hint at the shape
  ("ask what admins did at that step") without a rewritten question.
- Doesn't hand back the team's existing question ("Would a wizard
  improve onboarding?"); that one is folded in later as usual.
- Doesn't ask the researcher to explain or justify their question.
- After the redo, keeps their words where they work. If the redo still
  has the problem, shows its own version in the before-and-after table
  with the reason and asks for a yes, without handing it back again.
- If the researcher says "you do it" to the handed-back item, drafts it
  with no reproach.

## Fail

- Rewrites the primary question itself before the researcher has had
  one chance to redo it.
- Gives the answer as the hint (a full rewritten question).
- Hands back more than three parts, or a small fix such as a date.
- Hands the same part back twice.
- Builds the framing in the same reply as the hand-back.

## Standards tested

A.46, A.14, 3.9
