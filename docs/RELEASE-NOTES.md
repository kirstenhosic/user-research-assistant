# Release notes

What's new in each version of Magpie, in plain words. Each release on
GitHub shows the entry for its date. The full detail of every change is
in the changelog (`docs/CHANGELOG.md`).

How to write an entry: a few short bullets about what changed for the
person using Magpie. Leave out file names, test names, rule numbers,
pull request numbers and other tools. Keep credits to the people whose
ideas Magpie uses.

## 2026-11-18

- Release notes are now short and in plain words, like these. When you
  ask Magpie which version you have, it points you here.

## 2026-11-17

- Magpie now asks you to do more of the thinking. When something you
  wrote has a problem that would change the result, Magpie names it,
  gives you a hint and asks you to try again. If it still needs work,
  Magpie then shows its own version and why.
- Before Magpie drafts the study's framing, you write what you'd expect
  to see if your main hypotheses were wrong.
- You pick the research method and say why, before Magpie suggests one.
  If Magpie sees it differently, it shows its view next to yours. Your
  call stands.
- Magpie can raise up to three challenges at a time (five if you ask it
  to push you), most important first.

## 2026-11-12

- Themes keep their sharpest points. Before you review the themes,
  Magpie checks what each one may have smoothed over, such as a strong
  objection or a workaround, and keeps it visible. This idea comes from
  Nikki Anderson's newsletter.
- Answers don't quietly disappear. Magpie checks where the answers to
  each interview question ended up, and tells you about any that feed
  no finding.
- You can start at analysis. If your sessions are already done, Magpie
  asks what decision the study feeds and what a wrong call would cost.
- Magpie uses your research questions word for word and won't write
  over your edits to a file it made earlier.
- README: a new "What makes Magpie different" section, and setup tips
  for GitHub Copilot in VS Code.

## 2026-11-09

- When Magpie works from documents, such as product plans, past reports
  or web pages, each claim now names its source and quotes it, so you
  can check it quickly. A separate reviewer checks those claims before
  you see the summary.

## 2026-11-08

- Magpie tells you what it read. For each file, it shows where its
  reading ended, so you can spot a file that was cut short. If a
  transcript wasn't read in full, it stops and asks for the rest.
- After coding, Magpie checks that no part of any transcript was
  skipped.
- Each draft comes with a short list of the main choices behind it and
  why, so you can change any of them.
- If one of your answers is vague or unclear, Magpie asks a few
  follow-up questions before it drafts.
- Magpie judges web sources before it uses them. When your team's own
  material leaves a gap, it can offer a wider web search, and you
  decide.
- Every reply ends with a plain line saying Magpie is still on and
  which stage you're at.
- README: a new section on how Magpie helps your product team.

## 2026-11-05

- New help for showing the impact of your research, based on Carl J.
  Pearson's three levels of research impact. Magpie notes where your
  metric starts, offers a row for your own impact log at the end of a
  study, and has a new "impact check" you can start any time.

## 2026-11-04

- README: a link to the new intro video (voice by Prabarna Ganguly),
  and links that take you straight to the setup steps for your tool.

## 2026-11-03

- Better protection for personal details. Magpie checks every output
  meant for other people, not only reports. Participants' names and
  contact details are always left out, and company names are left out
  unless your team allows them inside the company.
- Magpie never asks for passwords or access keys, and leaves them out
  if they turn up in your material.
- When a study touches on a sensitive topic, such as health or money
  troubles, Magpie mentions it once.
- Reports now remind readers that AI can make mistakes, so quotes and
  findings should be checked against the raw material.
- Each release now includes a file of fingerprints (SHA256SUMS.txt), so
  you can check that a download is the real file.
- README: tips to get more from Magpie, a "Free and as is" note, and a
  way to report security problems.

## 2026-11-02

- Before your first transcript, Magpie reminds you once that what you
  paste goes to your AI tool as it is.
- What participants believe causes something is reported as their
  belief, never as a fact.
- README: a new section on privacy, security and personal details.

## 2026-11-01

- Your top findings always answer your main research question, or say
  plainly that the data didn't.
- More care with identity in analysis, such as gender, race, disability
  or age. It gets its own code when it matters to the study, and
  nothing is guessed.

## 2026-10-31

- The first public release of Magpie, with downloads for Claude, Gemini
  and Copilot.
- Each theme now explains why it happens, in participants' own words,
  and the findings list single voices worth noting.
- Magpie checks the independent review's points against your material
  before acting on them, and flags words that overclaim.
- For the findings that matter most, you say what would prove them
  wrong. This idea comes from claim-stress-tester by Kostas Kazakos.
- A new "fresh start" command moves a long study into a new
  conversation.
- You can ask Magpie which version it is.
- README: rewritten for people new to AI tools.
